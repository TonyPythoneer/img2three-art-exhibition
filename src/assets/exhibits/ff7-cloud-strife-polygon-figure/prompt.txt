# English Master Prompt — VII Polygon Soft Vinyl Figure: Cloud Strife

Two-stage build. Stage 1 sculpts ten isolated parts, each in its own agent, with **no integration**.
Stage 2 assembles them. Run Stage 1 to completion and review it before opening Stage 2.

References live in `references/` (white background trimmed, lossless WebP):

| file | view | note |
|---|---|---|
| `front.webp` (384×827) | orthographic front | primary proportion authority |
| `back.webp` (391×829) | orthographic back | back panels, hair rear dome |
| `left.webp` (214×847) | figure's **left** side | pauldron + grey bracer side |
| `right.webp` (206×831) | figure's **right** side | bare-shoulder side |
| `more-angle.webp` (297×584) | 3/4 front-right, warm studio light | strap/choker detail authority only |

Handedness is fixed and asymmetric — get it right before anything else:
**figure's LEFT** = black shoulder pauldron + grey forearm bracer + black fist.
**figure's RIGHT** = bare skin deltoid, no bracer, black fist only.

## BEGIN PROMPT

You are the lead agent for a code-only Three.js reconstruction of the **VII Polygon Soft Vinyl
Figure — Cloud Strife**, a faceted low-poly vinyl statue of the PS1 Final Fantasy VII field model.
The subject is a *toy*, not a character: hard flat facets, visible edge creases, matte vinyl,
zero smoothing groups. Do not "improve" it into a smooth anime figure — the faceting **is** the
identity.

### 0. Run the real img2threejs workflow

Use the checked-out img2threejs repository as runtime authority (`SKILL.md`).

```bash
python3 forge/state.py init \
  --state .img2threejs/state.json \
  --reference references/front.webp \
  --profile character
```

At every start, resume, and correction loop, before touching implementation code:

```bash
python3 forge/next.py --state .img2threejs/state.json object-sculpt-spec.json
```

Exit code `3` or `status=stopped` is a hard stop — report and request input. Never reconstruct
progress from chat memory. Use the repository's real `ObjectSculptSpec`, `detailInventory`,
`qualityContract`, sockets, materials, review history, and generated TypeScript `THREE.Group`
factory. Do not invent repository internals.

The `character` profile requires the character contracts and landmark evidence. The subject is a
stylized figurine, so record `anatomy` as **figurine proportions**, not human proportions, and say
so in the spec rationale.

Widths / depths, as a fraction of total height:

Read these three silhouette facts as hard gates:
1. The **hip is wider and deeper than the shoulders** — the pants are the largest volume in the model.
2. In profile the hip/pants read as a **kite/rhombus** with a sharp point front and back at Y≈0.318,
   not as a cylinder or a skirt.
3. The **torso is a thin slab** (depth 0.089 vs width 0.200), roughly 2.2:1 flat.

### 3. Stage 1 — ten isolated parts, no integration

Dispatch one agent per part. Each agent owns exactly one part and must not touch another's file.

Every part agent delivers:
- `src/exhibits/cloud-strife-polygon-figure/parts/create<Part>.ts`, exporting
  `create<Part>(options): THREE.Group`, built at the true normalized scale from §1;
- the part's own origin at its **socket point** (the joint where Stage 2 will attach it), listed
  below per part, so Stage 2 is pure placement with no re-centering;
- a named socket for every downstream attachment, as `group.userData.sockets = { name: Vector3 }`;
- a standalone render of the part from front + the relevant side + one 3/4 orbit, compared against
  the matching reference crop at ≥6× NEAREST zoom;
- a 猜測清單 entry for anything the references do not resolve: part / two readings / which was
  chosen / on what evidence.

**Do not integrate.** No part agent may create the assembled figure, a shared root group, or a
scene. Stage 2 does that.

---

**Part 1 — head** (`front.webp` y 0–250, `back.webp` y 50–350, both side views)

Two sub-volumes plus a face decal:

- *Hair cap*: a faceted dome, ~12–16 facets, closest to a low-subdivision icosahedron flattened
  slightly front-to-back. The back view shows a clean radial facet fan converging at the occiput
  with a shallow V crease down the centre-back. It covers the whole skull, the ears, and stops at
  a hard horizontal line at the nape.
- *Hair spikes*: 9–11 separate flat-sided wedges, each a 3–4 sided pyramid with a long thin
  triangular profile, welded into the cap. Layout from the references:
  one dominant spike leaning up-and-forward over the brow (the tallest, defines Y=1.000);
  2 more large spikes fanning up-and-back from the crown; 2 side spikes projecting laterally
  past the ears in the front view; 2–3 shorter rear spikes visible only in the back view;
  1–2 short forward fringe wedges hanging over the eyes down to Y≈0.760.
  Spikes are **not** rotationally symmetric — copy the reference layout, do not procedurally
  scatter them.
- *Head/face*: a rounded-off wedge, widest at the cheekbones, tapering to a small pointed chin.
  The face plane is nearly flat. Ears are small skin wedges just visible under the hair cap in
  the front view.
- *Face decal*: the eyes, brows, and mouth line are **printed flat art**, not geometry. Build them
  as a `CanvasTexture` (`colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`) mapped to the front face plane, or as a
  projected crop of `front.webp` de-lit via `stage1_intake/delight_albedo.py`. Do not model
  eyeballs. The eyes are large, angular, blue (`#3D6382` iris, white sclera, hard black lash line),
  brows are thin dark angular strokes. Chin: 0.700; eye line: ~0.790.
- *Neck*: a short skin column, width 0.035, from 0.673 to 0.700, plus a thin brown choker band at
  its base (clearest in `more-angle.webp`).

Socket origin: neck base, at (0, 0.673, 0).

---

**Part 2 — left shoulder** / **Part 3 — right shoulder** (`front.webp` y 195–365, both side views)

Asymmetric. Build them as two separate parts, not a mirror pair.

- *Right shoulder (figure's right, bare)*: a skin deltoid plate. In the side view it reads as a
  **pentagon** — flat outer face, chamfered top, tapering to the upper-arm socket at the bottom.
  It sits proud of the torso, wider than the upper arm below it. No sleeve, no cap.
- *Left shoulder*: the same skin deltoid, **plus** a black pauldron cap sitting over its top and
  outer face. The pauldron is a chunky beveled quad — flat top, flat outer face, chamfered leading
  and trailing edges, clearly a separate shell floating a hair above the deltoid with a visible
  gap line. In `back.webp` it wraps onto the upper back and is met by a dark olive-brown triangular
  strap panel crossing the shoulder blade diagonally.

Socket origins: shoulder joint centre, at (±0.100, 0.660, 0). Socket out: `upperArm`.

---

**Part 4 — left back arm** / **Part 5 — right back arm** (upper arms; side views are authority)

Both identical, mirrored. A narrow skin bar, roughly square in section with chamfered corners,
markedly thinner than both the deltoid above and the forearm below. It runs **down and slightly
forward**: about 10–15° of forward lean off vertical in the side view, and about 8° outward in
the front view. Length ≈ 0.115.

Socket origins: shoulder end. Socket out: `elbow`.

---

**Part 6 — left front arm** / **Part 7 — right front arm** (forearm + hand; `F8`/`F9` crops)

The forearm is a **wider, deeper block than the upper arm** — a tapered slab that widens as it
descends, angled forward and inward so the fists sit ahead of the hips. That forward-broken elbow
is a signature of the pose; a straight hanging arm is a fail.

- *Right front arm*: skin forearm → hard horizontal seam → **black fist**, a single chamfered
  cuboid with no finger geometry. The seam is a clean flat cut, not a taper.
- *Left front arm*: skin forearm → **grey bracer** (a slightly wider cuboid segment, ~0.045 tall)
  → hard seam → **black fist**. The bracer's outer face is flat; its lower edge overhangs the
  fist slightly in the side view.

Socket origins: elbow. Socket out: `fist` (Stage 2 may want a weapon anchor there).

---

**Part 8 — body** (torso; `F5`, `B3`, `A2` crops)

A flat faceted slab, chamfered at every vertical edge, from the shoulder line (0.673) down to the
belt (0.546–0.513). Front and back are near-flat planes; the sides are narrow chamfers.

On the front:
- two vertical shoulder straps, ~0.030 wide, brown, running from the shoulder line down to the
  belt, spaced to leave a purple centre panel between them. They are raised strips with a real
  thickness step, not painted-on;
- the straps continue **over** the shoulder line and onto the back, where they read as diagonal
  triangular panels crossing the shoulder blades;
- the waist **belt** is a wide brown band, 0.033 tall, wrapping fully around, standing proud of
  the shirt with a hard step above and below.

The shirt's lower hem sits inside the belt; the belt is the widest thing on the torso.

Socket origin: waist centre, at (0, 0.513, 0). Sockets out: `neck`, `shoulderL`, `shoulderR`, `hip`.

---

**Part 9 — legs** (pants + legs; `F10`, `F11`, `L2` crops)

One continuous volume, not two trousers. This is the hardest part and the one that decides the
silhouette.

- From the belt down, a **hip yoke** panel with a downward-pointing **chevron/V tip** at 0.435,
  present on the front *and* the back, sitting proud of the pant body with a visible step.
- Below the yoke, the pants flare outward and forward/backward to the widest point at 0.318,
  where the profile is a **kite**: sharp point front, sharp point back, max depth 0.142, max
  width 0.278. Facets read as a small number of large flat planes with hard creases; there is a
  vertical crease running down the centre of each thigh in the front view.
- The volume then contracts sharply to the **crotch arch**, an inverted-V notch whose apex is at
  0.268 — this is where the two legs separate.
- Below 0.268, two **bare tapered columns** (still purple, still pants) drop almost straight to
  the boot cuffs at 0.173, tapering slightly and splaying outward about 5°.

Socket origin: waist centre, at (0, 0.513, 0). Sockets out: `ankleL`, `ankleR` at (±0.055, 0.173, 0).

---

**Part 10 — shoes** (`F12`, `L3` crops)

Two pieces per foot, and the wedge is the whole character of it:

- *Boot cuff*: an olive-brown cuboid with chamfered vertical edges, from 0.173 down to ~0.085,
  swallowing the bottom of the pant column with a visible overlap step.
- *Sole slab*: a flat wedge under the cuff. It extends **forward** a long way (total foot length
  0.148, with roughly ⅔ of that ahead of the ankle) and **backward** only a short heel. Its top
  face steps out from under the cuff as a clean ledge; the toe end is a blunt chamfered wedge, not
  a point. Thickness ≈ 0.055, uniform — it is a slab, not a curved sole.
- Feet splay outward ~25–30° in plan view. They are **not** parallel.

Socket origins: ankle top centre. Build both feet in this one part, mirrored.

### 4. Stage 1 gates

Per part, before it may be marked done:

1. `forge/stage4_review/diagnose_render.py` Tier 1 passes, recorded into the spec with
   `--pass-id <pass> --in-place`.
2. `diagnose_render_multi_angle.py` on the fixed view plus ≥2 orbit views — every part here is
   non-planar.
3. `make_comparison_sheet.py` against the matching reference crop, inspected with agent vision at
   ≥6× NEAREST. An unzoomed comparison does not count as having looked.
4. `append_review.py` with per-feature scores. A part fails if any identity-defining feature is
   wrong even when the global score passes — specifically: handedness, the hip kite profile, the
   forward-broken elbow, the spike layout, the sole wedge.
5. Flat shading is on and no facet has been smoothed away.
6. The part is explodable and clickable on its own.

Correction budget: 3 per pass, 6 total, per the local state. Reaching either is a hard stop.

### 5. Stage 2 — integration

Only after all ten parts pass. Do not start early.

1. Build `createCloudStrifePolygonFigureModel.ts` as a pure assembly: import the ten part
   factories, attach each at its socket, apply the pose rotations below. **No geometry authored
   in this file.** If a part needs a shape change, it goes back to the part file.
2. Pose, off the neutral part orientations:
   - upper arms: 8° outward, 12° forward;
   - forearms: broken forward at the elbow so the fists land ahead of the hip line, level with
     0.400–0.450;
   - feet: ±27° yaw outward;
   - head: neutral, level gaze;
   - the figure stands with legs slightly apart, weight even, feet flat on Y=0.
3. Constant ownership: the integration file owns **only** socket positions and pose rotations.
   Every dimension from §1 stays owned by the part that uses it. Shape work can run in parallel;
   integration cannot.
4. Whole-figure gates: silhouette IoU against all four orthographic views (front, back, left,
   right), part coverage via `check_part_coverage.py --manifest parts.json`, and an explode /
   click check on the assembled model.
5. Wire the exhibit into the four places `CLAUDE.md` requires: `src/exhibits/index.ts`,
   `src/pages/[slug].vue` `STAGES`, `src/components/home/heroStage.ts` `heroEntries()`, and a
   cover image + `prompt.txt` at the **exhibit root** (the home-page glob only scans direct
   children — files under `references/` are invisible to it).
6. Write `spec/RELATIONSHIPS.md`: the ten parts, their sockets, the pipeline order, and which
   constants each part owns.
