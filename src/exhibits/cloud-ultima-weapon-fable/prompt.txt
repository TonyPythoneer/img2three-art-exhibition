/img2threejs Rebuild the weapon in the attached reference image as a procedural,
code-only Three.js model.

Subject:
Cloud's Ultima Weapon from the original 1997 Final Fantasy VII.

Reference authority:
Treat the attached image as the primary and authoritative visual reference.

Do not substitute or mix in designs from:
- Final Fantasy VII Remake or Rebirth
- Dissidia
- Kingdom Hearts
- Ever Crisis
- fan-made redesigns
- the Buster Sword
- the Fusion Sword

The target is the low-polygon original FFVII weapon shown in the supplied image.

Overall visual direction:
Preserve the original PS1-era faceted construction, angular silhouette,
low polygon count, proportions, colour placement and translucent layered blade.

Do not redesign it as a realistic forged sword.
Do not make the geometry smooth, ornamental or highly detailed.
Do not add unsupported engravings, screws, runes, mechanical seams or decorations.

Coordinate system:
- local +Y points from the handle toward the blade tip
- local -Y points toward the pommel
- local +X points to the weapon's right side
- local +Z points toward the visible front face
- place the assembled weapon origin at the guard and handle junction

Atomic component requirement:
This is a compound hard-surface object.

Do not build the entire weapon as one mesh.
Do not merge visually separate pieces merely to reduce component count.

Every independently visible or mechanically plausible piece must be constructed
as its own named THREE.Mesh or THREE.Group.

Geometry requirements:

1. Outer blade shell

Construct the pale outer blade as a custom faceted polygonal volume.

It has:
- a very long asymmetrical leaf-like silhouette
- a narrow pointed tip
- a straighter left edge
- a strongly curved and widening right edge
- a broad base that narrows into the guard
- a thin but visible three-dimensional thickness
- deliberately low-poly planar surfaces

Do not use a simple BoxGeometry or a smooth capsule.
Use a custom polygon profile, extruded into a shallow volume.

Keep the front and back shell surfaces independently addressable.

The shell should appear translucent milky white with a faint cool lavender tint.
The centre should remain transparent enough for the purple inner blade to remain
clearly visible through it.

2. Purple inner energy blade

Construct the purple inner blade as a completely independent polygonal volume.

It should:
- begin just above the guard
- occupy roughly the centre-lower portion of the outer blade
- stop well before the outer blade tip
- taper upward
- have a dark navy-violet upper region
- transition into brighter saturated violet toward its lower end
- remain visually separated from the outer translucent shell

Use a simple procedural vertical colour gradient.
Do not bake the purple section into the outer blade material.

3. Magenta central spine

Create the narrow red-magenta spear-like element visible in front of the
purple core near the guard.

It should be an independent thin angular component with:
- a long pointed upper tip
- a wider lower diamond or arrowhead region
- a dark burgundy upper section
- a brighter magenta-red lower section
- slight separation from the purple core to prevent z-fighting

4. Guard structure

Build the guard as a collection of separate angular low-poly blocks.

The guard contains:
- one central brown-gold polygonal hub
- one dark gunmetal shoulder block on each side
- angular gold or olive stabilizer blocks beneath the shoulders
- six assumed crimson emitter rods, three on each side

The emitter rods should:
- radiate outward from the central guard
- use low-segment faceted cylinders
- have a dark red-to-magenta lacquered surface
- have darker ends
- remain individual selectable nodes

The reference does not reveal all rods clearly.
Assume three mirrored rods on each side, but mark the hidden rods as inferred
with medium or low confidence.

5. Handle

Construct the handle as separate axial components:
- internal tang
- short neck below the guard
- long black grip core
- slightly wider black grip sleeve
- small pommel collar
- small golden pommel tip

Use an 8-sided or similarly low-sided cylindrical profile.
Do not produce a perfectly smooth modern grip.

Materials:

Create independent named materials:

  translucent milky white
  cool lavender tint
  moderate transmission
  low opacity
  low-to-medium roughness
  subtle internal depth
  no glass-like crystal refraction

- purpleCoreMaterial
  violet gradient
  slightly emissive
  mostly opaque
  brighter near the lower portion
  darker navy-purple near the upper portion

- magentaSpineMaterial
  dark burgundy to saturated magenta
  low roughness
  weak emissive response

- gunmetalGuardMaterial
  dark blue-grey
  medium roughness

  slightly olive

  high enough roughness to avoid polished jewellery appearance

- gripMaterial
  nearly black
  very low metalness
  medium-high roughness

Transparency handling:
The purple core must remain readable through the pale shell from front,
back and three-quarter views.

Use separate geometry volumes and stable transparency ordering.
Avoid z-fighting and broken transparency sorting.
Configure renderOrder and depthWrite deliberately where needed.

Detail inventory:
Before generating code, scan the reference by component zones.

Record every visible:
- silhouette change
- angular transition
- colour boundary
- translucent region
- bevel or chamfer
- exposed edge
- separate guard block
- emitter rod
- material transition

Every recorded detail must map to a real component, local feature or material
override. Do not leave identity-defining details only as prose.

Inference policy:
The reference supplies only one visible angle.

For hidden regions:
- mirror visible left/right structures where mechanically plausible
- mirror front/back blade thickness where plausible
- preserve the same low-poly visual language
- do not invent new decorative features
- mark every inferred component with confidence and inferenceReason
- report conflicts instead of silently guessing

Runtime hierarchy:
Expose all important components through:

root.userData.sculptRuntime = {
  nodes,
  sockets,
  materials,
  setExplode,
  resetAssembly,
  setPartVisible,
  getPartMetadata
}

Each atomic component must:
- have a stable unique name
- be stored in the nodes map
- include userData.partId
- include userData.category
- include userData.confidence
- include userData.evidenceType as "visible", "mirrored" or "inferred"
- include userData.assembledTransform
- include userData.explodeVector

Required sockets:
- bladeSocket
- guardSocket
- handleSocket
- pommelSocket
- leftEmitterSocket
- rightEmitterSocket

Exploded-view behaviour:
Implement:

setExplode(amount: number): void

where amount is clamped from 0 to 1.

At amount 0:
- the weapon is completely assembled

At amount 1:
- every major component is visibly separated
- the original orientation is preserved
- no component rotates randomly
- the relationship between parts remains understandable

Explosion directions:
- front blade layers move toward +Z
- back blade layers move toward -Z
- left guard parts move toward -X
- right guard parts move toward +X
- emitter rods move radially outward from the guard
- purple core moves slightly toward +Z
- magenta spine moves farther toward +Z
- upper blade mount pieces move slightly toward +Y
- handle components separate sequentially toward -Y
- pommel components move farthest toward -Y

Use each component's original assembled transform as the source of truth.
Do not accumulate transform errors when the explode amount changes repeatedly.

Also implement:

setPartVisible(partId: string, visible: boolean): void

This must allow the showcase UI to hide or isolate individual pieces.

Animation:
Add a slow optional display rotation through root.userData.tick.

Do not animate the individual components while the model is assembled.
The exploded state should be controlled only through setExplode().

Quality gates:
Run strict quality validation.

Do not advance from blockout until:
- the outer silhouette matches the reference
- the blade-to-handle length ratio matches
- the guard width matches
- the purple core placement matches
- the weapon reads correctly as a small thumbnail

Do not advance from structural pass until:
- every atomic component exists
- no major part is fused into an unrelated part
- all attachments touch correctly
- mirrored and inferred parts are recorded

Do not advance from material pass until:
- the purple core remains visible through the pale shell
- the shell does not become fully invisible
- the shell does not look like clear household glass
- the gold, gunmetal, crimson and black regions remain distinct
- tone mapping does not remove the violet colour

- a named node inventory
- a confidence report for inferred regions
- assembled and exploded comparison renders

Use only procedural Three.js geometry and generated textures.
Do not import GLB, OBJ, FBX or downloaded mesh assets.
